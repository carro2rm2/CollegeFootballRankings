How this version ranks teams

Division hierarchy: Power-conference FBS teams (and Notre Dame) rank ahead of other FBS, followed by FCS, Division II and Division III. A lower-division team can jump a higher-division opponent only if it has a winning head-to-head record against that specific team. Without a direct result, the division hierarchy holds.

Notre Dame: Notre Dame receives the same top-tier treatment as a Power Conference FBS team. Its ranking is then determined by its actual schedule and results, not a conference label.

Head-to-head: When two teams have records within one win, a decisive head-to-head result is the first major tiebreaker. If the relevant results form a circular chain, the model reduces the direct head-to-head bonus and uses the recency of those games instead.

Recent form: Recent games count more than early-season games. A current winning streak receives an additional form bonus, allowing a hot 6–4 team to make a meaningful move without erasing its earlier losses.

Résumé and consistency: Quality wins earn more credit, losses to weak opponents cost more, and every game still contributes. Margin of victory is capped and used as a final dominance tiebreaker rather than allowing blowouts to overwhelm the résumé.

Opponent quality is estimated from an iterative schedule rating. Cross-division comparisons remain less certain when teams have few or no common opponents. This is an independent model, not an official poll.
